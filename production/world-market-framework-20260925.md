## Usable planning layout proposal — 25 September 2026

User asks whether the framework should be a week-by-week table filled in stages. Proposed: one concise year overview with rows for experiences spanning a week/range (multiple experiences per week allowed), linked to expandable detailed records, not a second master. Overview columns: week/range, journey chapter, experience/purpose, learning objectives and curriculum role, evidence/success check, status/open decision. Engagement intention lives within purpose; do not treat it as competing with curriculum. Three design passes: (1) intent and evidence, including purpose, objectives, curriculum role and how success will be recognised; (2) student/teaching sequence and game/world details, choices, feedback, rewards, support; (3) feasibility/responsible delivery and verification, specifying minimal artifacts, teacher view, data handling, accessibility and test results. Passes are iterative, not waterfall: known privacy/accessibility constraints apply from the start, and evidence is planned before gameplay. Source links at domain headings provide shared references; each material choice needs its specific rationale/source link. Not every domain must be populated with a feature. No new authoritative timetable, approval, runtime, asset, data collection or publication. This is a layout proposal for review, not an implemented reader UI.

## Curriculum precision: what this market can and cannot show
- **EB-01, concept of initiative:** enacted behaviour plus consolidation can support teaching. Completion alone does not prove the student understands the concept.
- **EB-02, ways to show initiative:** improving work practices and proposing a change are plausible practice links. Helping colleagues and seeking additional responsibility need distinct evidence; do not mark all five bullets complete from one badge.
- **EB-03, benefits:** improved service and recognition provide examples for discussion. Receiving a reward does not prove understanding of every listed benefit.
- **ES-PROB-01, open/closed questioning:** clicking supplied dialogue is not sufficient evidence that a learner can formulate and use both types.
- **ES-PROB-02, generate possible solutions:** configuring supplied alternatives is useful practice but not equivalent to independently generating a list.
- **ES-PROB-03, decision-making model:** planning/testing/review provides a candidate link. Make the model and learner use explicit before claiming this point is demonstrated.

These are mappings against the retained curriculum ledger, not a fresh official syllabus compliance audit.

## Teacher-facing interpretation example (proposed)
“Revised helper role after observing fewer bags ready; improved pickup but left an ordering delay.”
Useful next action: ask the learner what caused the trade-off and what they would try under a different demand.

This is more informative than “three attempts, badge earned.” Attempts may reflect exploration, uncertainty or interface difficulty; support use is not a failure. Do not derive competence automatically from speed, clicks, money or badges.

## What the framework test exposes
The existing market connects world, teaching, decisions and rewards reasonably well in design records. Its main unresolved educational joins are exact curriculum evidence, transfer and the teacher's interpretation. Current guided reflection is a deliberate response constraint, but narrows what that artifact can demonstrate. Treat this as a design trade-off, not silently restore free text or claim guided choices prove independent reasoning.

Next review should check whether the opening achieves introduction and quick wins, then its relationship to the next chapter. Any retained learning evidence needs a specific purpose; formal assessment need not be attached here. Avoid attaching every world system or curriculum requirement to this first experience. Community governance, global scope and flourishing can be introduced later.
