Detailed experience and teaching design

Start with one place and a problem students can influence: Sunday Markets. Give the wider world a brief glimpse, then let students get to know Mara, Sam and their own character by doing useful work. A whole-campus scavenger tour delays the first reason to care and makes movement look like achievement. A focused encounter gives a memorable person, a manageable decision and a consequence to return to; the wider world opens as questions develop.

Use Tania’s welcome v4, 62.625 seconds, as the opening invitation. It connects strengths, future choices and life beyond school to Career Empire and includes actual campus footage. The older Student Intro/Explainer/Final videos show legacy dashboards and progression claims. V4 is the strongest candidate, with captions, transcript and reduced motion; final audio/synchronisation acceptance and installation remain pending. Replace duplicate course-purpose cards with this invitation, keeping a short initiative explanation at the moment it is needed. Avoid stacking the video, the current 14-second flyover and all five welcome cards.

The connected story is: “What kind of life might my character build?” → “Can I make this shift work better?” → “What changed, and who did it help?” → “If work is changing, which opportunities should I investigate?” Week 2 returns to the character’s Market experience, then Nora helps investigate a provisional next lead. Choosing a lead does not commit a student to a real career.

Day Zero: first 15–20 minutes of Week 1, double A

This is part of the first lesson, not an extra session. Times are design estimates, not countdowns or assessment conditions.

Approximate timeStudent experience and purpose
0–2 minutesTeacher gives the premise: “You’ll look after a character, make choices about work and life, and learn how to make better decisions. Money shows one consequence; your reasoning is assessed separately.” Play welcome v4, or read the transcript; allow skip/replay.
2–5 minutesUse the existing/default character or optional helper selection. Invite a fictional aspiration—such as learning a skill, helping the neighbourhood or finding satisfying work—without collecting family, health or financial disclosures. Appearance choice is optional and can be revisited; avoid starting a long wardrobe session.
5–9 minutesA short walk towards Mara teaches only move/look/interact, help and exit. One interaction is enough. Offer “I can move—take me to Mara”, movement assistance, or a teacher-led screenshot/tabletop route. Students may look around; there is no mandatory waypoint list, visit reward or timed penalty. The proposed first-visit skip still needs implementation verification.
9–15 minutesMara offers a small paid trial and a concrete need: ordering, collection and packing are getting in each other’s way. Meet Sam and observe one customer journey. Teach initiative as noticing and taking a useful action within the role, checking permission and workload. Show how to reopen teaching, pause and ask for help.
15–20 minutesEach learner chooses what to investigate and states a prediction or asks a clarifying question. “Can I change the signs?” is different from “What makes customers queue in the wrong place?” Let both matter. Begin the problem; complete the teaching and trial during the fortnight.

Care for the learner’s own character starts with a chosen direction: learn a useful skill, save the first earnings towards a fictional personal goal, help make the neighbourhood work better, or propose another direction. The learner chooses what matters in this first shift: learning a new role, improving Sam’s workload or helping customers. That changes the improvement they trial and the evidence they look for. Week 2’s lead is chosen for that same character, weighing what they want against available work, training, distance and conditions. It is their continuing decision, not merely completion of Mara’s job.

“Remembered result” means returning to the learner’s chosen goal, plan, observed effect and next lead. Use these within the two school-held evidence cards, then begin the next double from that checkpoint. An own sign/queue/handover trial is explicitly teacher-facilitated; the class can retain/test the learner’s improvement at the next Market role-play. The game currently remembers some appearance and browser-local Market plans/pay/trials, but Market state is not reliably owned by each character, and research leads reset. A later minimal continuity connection is proposed; no new character-care meters or expanded world system is assumed. Attachment remains a hypothesis to test with students.
