## The player promise

You are starting to make a life here. Try something, work with people, respond when plans change, earn something and decide what matters to you. The opening should make the actual Careers and Employability experience appealing. It must not promise an unrelated adventure game.

## First playable sequence

| Beat | What the student does | What the world does |
|---|---|---|
| Arrive | Enters with their chosen avatar or available default; follows a visible stall marker | A small market is opening. Decorative stalls and a modest music stage suggest a larger event. A short optional camera moment hands over control immediately. |
| Meet your contact | Talks to one stallholder; accepts a clearly explained short paid shift | Stallholder offers a simple responsibility: help customers find the collection point. Base pay is stated before accepting. Player can explore and return rather than being forced into the job. |
| Notice the problem | Looks at the stall and speaks to a waiting customer | The collection sign is missing; people cannot tell where to go. The goal is to make the collection point clear, not to guess an invisible correct answer. |
| Try an approach | Clarifies what customers need, positions a temporary sign, or asks a helper to direct people | Sign position and customer movement respond. More than one workable response is possible. Only a few lightweight character movements are needed in the first build. |
| Use feedback | If the sign is unclear, sees a customer still hesitate and can reposition it or try help | Brief specific feedback explains what remains confusing. No compulsory failure, hidden punishment or lost base pay for an honest supported attempt. |
| See your contribution | Watches the collection point work and finishes the agreed task | A few customers reach it; the stallholder acknowledges the particular action. A communication or problem-solving badge names the in-game behaviour. |
| Finish work, enjoy the place | Receives first pay and chooses to save, browse a small keepsake, or enjoy the music for free | Balance changes once. A purchased keepsake appears in a bounded personal display; free enjoyment stays available. Another small opportunity is hinted at. |
| Return later | Leaves and reopens the experience | The same practice save recalls task state, pay, badges and owned item. No login required and no absence penalties. |

Working duration: roughly 5–8 minutes for the guided loop, with optional exploration. Test the duration rather than imposing a countdown.

## What this actually tests

- Existing avatar movement, interaction and returning from a panel to the world.
- A small change in circumstances that the player can understand and influence.
- Communication and problem-solving recognition based on defined actions, not every click.
- Feedback and another approach without a permanent failure state.
- A single payment, explicit balance, saving and one optional purchase with a visible consequence.
- Persistent local state, including resume before and after payment without duplicate rewards.
- Enjoyment and relationships that are not all bought with money.

Game badges remain separate from real-world portfolio evidence, portfolio points, assessed mastery and résumé claims. The first version needs only a few badge examples; broader recognition can grow across the year. The first pay display may show fictional gross/tax/net if that rule is implemented, but the opening does not need a tax lesson or community-fund tutorial.

A successful opening test does not prove year-long engagement.
